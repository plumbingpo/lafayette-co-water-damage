Crawl Space Water Removal in Lafayette

Water under the house is easy to miss. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Signs to Look For

  • Hardwood boards upstairs starting to cup, with edges higher than the centers
  • Dark water marks and chalky deposits partway up the block or concrete
  • Insulation that has pulled loose and hangs in wet clumps below the joists
  • Standing water or mud on the ground when you open the access hatch
  • Corroded metal such as duct straps, pipe hangers and steel posts
  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

Day one

Water Pools Below

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Joists and sill plates soften, fasteners rust and the damp space attracts termites and other pests.

Our Process

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Before a technician goes under the house, we look for live electrical dangers, fallen ductwork and dirty water. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Remove Standing Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Dry and Verify

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Water That Is Not Clean

Do not assume the water below your floor is clean. If it came from a drain pipe or from waste laden groundwater it is rated Category 2 or 3, so we suit up and disinfect.

Termites and Other Pests

Rodents, carpenter ants and termites all seek out moist lumber and soggy ground. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

Settling Under the House

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Electrical runs, junction boxes and ductwork pass through most crawl spaces. Wires under water can shock someone, and wet duct wrap pushes a moldy smell through every vent.
